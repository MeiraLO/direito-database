# Writing
> IELTS Academic Writing — Task 1 and Task 2

---

## Overview

| Task | Time | Words | Score Weight |
|------|------|-------|-------------|
| Task 1 | ~20 min | min. 150 | 1/3 of Writing score |
| Task 2 | ~40 min | min. 250 | 2/3 of Writing score |

> ⚠️ Always do **Task 2 first** — it's worth more marks.

---

## Task 1 — Academic (Describe a Visual)

### Possible visual types:
- Line graph · Bar chart · Pie chart · Table
- Process diagram · Map (comparison) · Flow chart

### Structure:
1. **Introduction** — paraphrase the question (what the graph shows, time period, units)
2. **Overview** — 2–3 most important trends (no data — just the big picture)
3. **Details paragraph 1** — specific data with comparisons
4. **Details paragraph 2** — more specific data

### Essential Phrases

**Introducing:**
- The graph/chart/table illustrates / shows / compares / represents...
- The diagram provides information about...
- According to the data...

**Describing trends (line graphs):**
- rose sharply / gradually / steadily
- fell dramatically / slightly / significantly
- remained stable / constant / unchanged
- fluctuated throughout the period
- peaked at... / reached a low of... / levelled off at...
- there was a sharp rise / a gradual decline in...

**Comparing:**
- compared to / in contrast to / whereas / while
- the most significant difference was...
- X was twice as high as Y / X was considerably higher than Y
- at ... times as many ... as ...

**Approximate data:**
- approximately / roughly / around / about / just over / just under / nearly

### Tenses:
- Past data → past simple: "Sales **rose** in 2010."
- Predictions → will / is expected to: "Numbers **are projected to increase**."
- Process diagrams → present simple passive: "The water **is filtered** first."

## Task 2 — Essay

### Common question types:

| Type | What it asks | Your job |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Opinion (agree/disagree) | To what extent do you agree? | State a clear position; argue it consistently |
| Discussion | Discuss both views | Present both sides fairly; give YOUR view |
| Problem/Solution | Causes and solutions | Identify causes, suggest solutions |
| Advantages/Disadvantages | Discuss pros and cons | Balanced analysis; may need to say which outweighs |
| Two-part question | Directly asks two questions | Answer BOTH questions explicitly |

---

### Essay Structure (4 paragraphs)

#### Introduction
- Paraphrase the question topic (1–2 sentences)
- State your position or what the essay will cover (1 sentence)
- **Do NOT simply copy** the question word-for-word.

#### Body Paragraph 1
- Topic sentence (main point)
- Explanation / development
- Example or evidence
- (Optional: concession / counterpoint)

#### Body Paragraph 2
- Topic sentence (second main point or opposing view)
- Explanation / development
- Example or evidence

#### Conclusion
- Summarise your main points
- Restate your overall position
- Do NOT introduce new ideas

---

### Useful Essay Phrases

**Introducing your opinion:**
- In my opinion, / In my view, / I believe that / I would argue that / It seems to me that
- From my perspective, / As I see it,

**Presenting arguments:**
- One of the main advantages of X is that...
- A significant drawback of this approach is...
- There is little doubt that... / It is widely accepted that...
- Proponents of X argue that... / Critics of X claim that...

**Conceding a point:**
- While it is true that... / Admittedly, X has some merit.
- Although X may be beneficial, the disadvantages outweigh the benefits.
- It cannot be denied that... However, ...

**Giving examples:**
- For example, / For instance, / To illustrate this,
- A clear example of this is... / This is exemplified by...
- Take the case of... / Consider the example of...

**Concluding:**
- In conclusion, / To conclude, / To sum up,
- On balance, / Overall, / Taking everything into account,
- It is clear that / For these reasons, I believe that...

### Informal Letters — Key Features (B2 / Cambridge First)

**Opening:**
- Dear [First name], (NOT "Dear Friend,")

**Opening lines (engaging):**
- Thanks for your letter / It was great to hear from you!
- Sorry for not writing sooner, I've been really busy.

**Closing:**
- Best wishes, / Lots of love, / Take care, / Write back soon!

**Features of informal style:**
- Contractions: I'm, don't, it's, we've, I'd
- Colloquial expressions: loads of fun / ages ago / I reckon / going on
- Direct questions: "How are things? / What do you think?"
- Conversational linkers: Anyway, / By the way, / Oh, and / Actually,

---

### Reports — Key Features (Cambridge First / IELTS Academic)

**Format:**
- Title / Heading
- Introduction: purpose of report
- Sections with sub-headings
- Conclusion and/or Recommendations

**Tone:** formal and impersonal
- Use passive: *It was found that / It is recommended that*
- Avoid contractions and personal pronouns where possible
- Use impersonal structures: *The majority of respondents / The data suggests*

---

### Essays — Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Correction |
|---------|-----------|
| Starting every sentence with "I" | Vary with "It is / There is / This shows..." |
| Very general statements | Be specific; use examples |
| One giant paragraph | Use clear paragraph breaks |
| Repeating the same word | Use synonyms and pronoun reference |
| Off-topic sentences | Every sentence must support the topic sentence |
| Too short (under 250 words) | Loses marks — always count |
| Informal language in Task 2 | Use formal register throughout |

---

### Useful Sentence Patterns for Academic Writing

- **Cleft sentence for emphasis:** *It is the government that must take responsibility.*
- **Passive for objectivity:** *Considerable damage has been caused by pollution.*
- **Participle clause for concision:** *Facing increasing pressure, companies have been forced to adapt.*
- **Nominalization:** *The introduction of new technology → the introduction of new technology (not "when they introduced...")* 
- **Hedging language:** *This may suggest / It could be argued / There appears to be*
