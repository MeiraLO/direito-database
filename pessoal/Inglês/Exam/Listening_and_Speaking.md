# Listening
> IELTS Listening — 4 Sections, 40 Questions, ~30 minutes + 10 min transfer

---

## Overview

| Section | Setting | Difficulty |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Everyday social dialogue (2 speakers) | Easiest |
| 2 | One person speaking on a general topic | Easy–Medium |
| 3 | Discussion between 2–4 speakers in academic context | Medium–Hard |
| 4 | Academic lecture or talk (1 speaker) | Hardest |

---

## Before You Listen

- Use the time given to **read questions in advance**
- Predict the type of answer (a name? a number? a place? an adjective?)
- **Underline keywords** in the questions
- Note how many words you can write ("NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER")

---

## While You Listen

- Answers follow the **order of the recording**
- Write answers as you hear them — don't go back while listening
- If you miss one, **move on immediately**
- Watch for **distractors** — speakers may mention something and then correct it: *"I'd like to book for Friday — actually, Saturday."*
- Numbers and spellings may be given out loud: "B-A-R-T-O-N"

---

## Question Types & Tips

### Form / Note / Table Completion
- Write the exact words you hear (check spelling)
- Numbers: write numerals (12, not twelve) unless it's part of a name
- Follow word limit strictly

### Multiple Choice
- Options may all be mentioned — choose only the ONE that answers the question
- Eliminate wrong answers as you hear them

### Matching
- Read all options before the section starts
- Options may be used more than once (or not at all)

### Map / Diagram Labelling
- Familiarise yourself with the layout during preparation time
- Follow directions: "Go past... turn left... it's opposite..."

### Sentence Completion
- Must be grammatically correct when complete
- Use exact words from the recording

---

## Common Listening Pitfalls

| Trap | Strategy |
|------|---------|
| Changed information ("or rather...") | Always write the final answer given |
| Spelled-out names/codes | Write each letter as you hear it |
| Distraction by wrong info | Listen for contrast words: "but", "actually", "however" |
| Mishearing numbers | 13 vs 30, 15 vs 50 — listen to stress pattern |
| Going back | Never go back — you'll miss the next answer |

---

## Useful Phrases to Recognise

**Redirecting / Correcting:**
- "Actually, I meant..." / "What I meant to say was..." / "Sorry, I should say..."

**Listing:**
- "First of all... then... finally..." / "There are three main points..."

**Emphasising:**
- "The most important thing is..." / "What you really need to remember..."

**Giving examples:**
- "For example, take the case of..." / "Such as..."

# Speaking
> IELTS Speaking — 3 Parts, 11–14 minutes, examiner face-to-face

---

## Overview

| Part | Format | Duration |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Introduction & questions on familiar topics | 4–5 min |
| 2 | Individual long turn (cue card + 1 min prep) | 3–4 min |
| 3 | Discussion of abstract/complex topics | 4–5 min |

**Assessed on:** Fluency & Coherence · Lexical Resource · Grammatical Range & Accuracy · Pronunciation

---

## Part 1 — Familiar Topics

**Topics:** your home, studies, work, hobbies, family, food, travel, technology, etc.

**Strategy:**
- Give **extended answers** — don't just say "Yes" or "No"
- Add a reason or example: "Yes, I really enjoy... because... For example, last week I..."
- Use discourse markers: *"Well, actually... / To be honest... / As a matter of fact..."*

**Model Answer Structure:**
> Direct answer → Reason / Explanation → Example or extra detail

---

## Part 2 — Long Turn (Cue Card)

**You have 1 minute to prepare and speak for 1–2 minutes.**

**Strategy:**
- Use your 1 minute to make brief notes on each bullet point
- Structure: **Introduction → main points → detail → conclusion/reflection**
- Keep talking — it's okay to expand beyond the bullet points
- Use past tense if describing a past event, present if general

**Useful Phrases:**
- "I'd like to talk about..."
- "This happened / took place..."
- "What I particularly remember is..."
- "The reason I chose this is..."
- "I'd definitely recommend it because..."
- "Looking back, I think..."

**Extend your answer with:**
- "...and what made it special was..."
- "...particularly because at that time..."
- "...which reminded me of..."

---

## Part 3 — Abstract Discussion

**Questions are more complex: opinions, causes, consequences, comparisons, predictions**

**Strategy:**
- Give full, developed answers — at least 3–4 sentences
- Show you can speculate: *"I think it's probably because... / It could be argued that..."*
- Compare and contrast: *"In contrast to... / This is quite different from..."*
- Use a range of grammar: conditionals, passives, modals, complex clauses

**Useful Discourse Markers:**

*Buying time (fluency):*
- "That's an interesting question..."
- "Well, let me think about that for a moment..."
- "It depends, really, on..."

*Expressing opinion:*
- "From my perspective..." / "As far as I'm concerned..."
- "I'd argue that..." / "I strongly believe..."
- "It seems to me that..."

*Hedging (being less certain):*
- "It's quite difficult to say, but I think..."
- "I might be wrong, but..." / "I'm not entirely sure, however..."
- "It could well be that..."

*Agreeing / Disagreeing with the examiner's implied point:*
- "Yes, I see what you mean. I'd also add that..."
- "That's a fair point, although I'd argue that..."
- "Actually, I'm not entirely convinced of that, because..."

---

## Pronunciation Tips

- **Word stress matters:** PHOtograph / phoTOGraphy / photoGRAPHic
- **Sentence stress:** emphasise content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs)
- **Intonation:** rising for questions / lists; falling for statements and completed thoughts
- **Linking sounds:** "an apple" → sounds like "anapple" | "went out" → "wentout"
- **Weak forms:** "to" → /tə/ | "and" → /ən/ | "a" → /ə/

## Topic Vocabulary for Speaking

### Describing a photo / place / person:
- In the foreground / background / on the left / in the centre
- It looks as if... / It seems like... / They appear to be...
- The atmosphere seems quite...
- What strikes me most is...

### Speculating:
- might / could / may → "They could be students..."
- probably / possibly / perhaps / apparently
- "I imagine that..." / "I would guess that..."

### Comparing photos (Cambridge First Speaking Part 2):
- Both photos show... / In contrast, the second photo...
- The main difference is... / They are similar in that...
- Whereas the first... / Unlike the first photo, the second...
- "If I had to choose, I'd prefer... because..."

## Cambridge First — Speaking Parts

| Part | Format |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Interview — questions on personal topics |
| 2 | Collaborative task — discuss and decide with a partner |
| 3 | Follow-up discussion with the examiner |
| 4 | Further discussion on related topics |

### Part 2 — Collaborative Task Phrases:
- "What do you think about...?"
- "Shall we decide on...?"
- "I agree with you on that, but..."
- "What if we...? / How about...?"
- "I see your point, but personally I think..."
- "So, are we agreed that...?"
