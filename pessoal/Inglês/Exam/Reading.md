# Reading
> IELTS Academic Reading — strategies by question type

---

## Overview

- **3 passages** with increasing difficulty
- **40 questions** in **60 minutes**
- Texts are from academic, scientific, or general-interest sources
- You do NOT need specialist knowledge — all answers are in the text

> ⚠️ Transfer answers directly to the answer sheet (no extra time given).

---

## General Strategy

1. **Skim** the passage quickly first (titles, headings, first/last sentences of paragraphs) — 2 min
2. **Read the questions** to know what to look for
3. **Scan** for specific information (names, dates, numbers, keywords)
4. Answer in **passage order** for most question types
5. If unsure, **move on and come back** — don't spend too long on one question

---

## Question Types

### True / False / Not Given
> This is one of the **hardest** types for many learners.

| Answer | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| TRUE | The statement matches what the text says |
| FALSE | The statement contradicts what the text says |
| NOT GIVEN | The information is not in the text (not mentioned or implied) |

> ⚠️ **NOT GIVEN ≠ FALSE.** If you can't find it, it's NOT GIVEN. Don't assume.
> ⚠️ Beware of **words that look the same** but are different in meaning.

**Strategy:**
- Find the part of the text that the statement refers to
- Compare the statement carefully — even one different word can change the answer
- Don't use outside knowledge

---

### Yes / No / Not Given (for writer's opinion/claims)
Same logic as T/F/NG but applied to **what the writer thinks**, not factual statements.

---

### Matching Headings
> Match each paragraph to the best heading from a list (there are always more headings than paragraphs).

**Strategy:**
- Read first and last sentences of each paragraph
- Identify the **main idea** of the paragraph (not just one detail)
- Eliminate obviously wrong headings first
- Headings paraphrase — they won't use the exact same words

---

### Multiple Choice
> Choose A, B, C, or D.

**Strategy:**
- Read the question stem carefully before looking at options
- Find the relevant part of the text
- Eliminate clearly wrong options
- Watch for **distractors** — options that use words from the text but with wrong meaning

---

### Matching Information / Features
> Match statements to a list (e.g., which paragraph contains which information, or match names to descriptions).

**Strategy:**
- Underline the key noun/concept in each statement
- Scan for those items in the text
- Answers may not follow text order

---

### Sentence / Summary Completion
> Fill in gaps in sentences or a summary using words **from the text**.

**Strategy:**
- Note the word limit (usually "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS")
- The completed sentence must be grammatically correct
- Use the exact words from the text — do not change the form

---

### Diagram / Flow Chart / Table Completion
Same principle as sentence completion — use words directly from the text.

## Key Vocabulary for Academic Texts

### Signposting language to recognise:
- **Contrast:** however / nevertheless / on the other hand / despite this / yet / conversely
- **Addition:** furthermore / moreover / in addition / not only... but also
- **Result:** therefore / consequently / as a result / thus / hence
- **Concession:** although / even though / while / whereas / admittedly
- **Emphasis:** in particular / above all / crucially / significantly / notably
- **Example:** for instance / such as / in particular / including
- **Summary:** in short / overall / in conclusion / to summarise

---

## Common Traps in IELTS Reading

| Trap | How to avoid |
|------|-------------|
| "Sounds right" but isn't in the text | Stick to what's written — no assumptions |
| Partial match (only half the statement is true) | The WHOLE statement must match |
| Absolute words (all, never, always) | Text must use the same extreme claim |
| Using outside knowledge | Only use information from the passage |
| Spending too long on one question | Skip and return — every question is worth 1 mark |

## Cambridge First (B2) — Reading & Use of English Parts

| Part | Type | Focus |
|------|------|-------|
| 1 | Multiple-choice cloze | Vocabulary (meaning, collocation, phrasal verbs) |
| 2 | Open cloze | Grammar and vocabulary |
| 3 | Word formation | Prefixes and suffixes |
| 4 | Key word transformations | Grammar + vocabulary (2 marks each) |
| 5 | Multiple choice (long text) | Detail, meaning, inference |
| 6 | Gapped text | Text structure, cohesion, coherence |
| 7 | Multiple matching | Finding specific information |

### Part 4 — Key Word Transformations (Strategy)
1. Read the first sentence carefully
2. Think about how the **key word** is commonly used grammatically
3. Write 2–5 words in the gap (contractions count as 2)
4. Do NOT change the key word
5. Check the second sentence means the same as the first

**Common structures tested:**
- Passive voice: "Someone stole my bag" → "My bag **was stolen**"
- Reported speech: "I'm tired," he said → "He said he **was tired**"
- Conditionals: "I won't go unless you come" → "I'll only go **if you come**"
- Comparatives: "No other student is as tall as Tom" → "Tom is **the tallest** student"
- Modal verbs: "I'm sure she's at home" → "She **must be** at home"
- Causative: "Someone is cutting her hair" → "She's **having her hair cut**"
