# Grammar
> Core grammar topics for IELTS B2–C1 (Cambridge Objective First)

---

## Comparison: Adjectives and Adverbs

**Comparative adjectives** compare two things.
- shorter than / more elegant than / less practical than

**Superlative adjectives** compare within a group.
- the tallest / the most talented / the least expensive

**as ... as** / **not as ... as** = equal or unequal comparison
- She is not as tall as her sister.
- These shoes are not as comfortable as flip flops.

**Adverbs of degree** intensify comparisons:
- far more / much less / a bit more / slightly less / considerably better

**Comparative adverbs:**
- quickly → more quickly | well → better | badly → worse

> 💡 *Memorise:* good → better → best | bad → worse → worst | far → farther/further → farthest/furthest

## Review of Present Tenses

| Tense | Use | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Present Simple | habits, facts, states | I study every day. |
| Present Continuous | actions in progress now; temporary | She is working from home. |
| Present Perfect | past with present relevance; unfinished time | I have lived here for 3 years. |
| Present Perfect Continuous | ongoing action started in the past | He has been studying since 9am. |

> 💡 *Key contrast:* "I have read 3 books" (completed) vs "I have been reading" (ongoing/emphasis on duration)

---

## Review of Past Tenses

| Tense | Use | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Past Simple | completed past actions | She left at 5pm. |
| Past Continuous | background action / interrupted action | I was cooking when she called. |
| Present Perfect | past with present relevance | I have never seen that film. |
| Past Perfect | the earlier of two past events | He had already eaten when I arrived. |

> ⚠️ *Common mistake:* Do NOT use past simple for a recent action that still has a result. Use present perfect. ✗ "I lost my keys." ✓ "I have lost my keys." (if you still don't have them)

## Modals 1 — Obligation, Necessity and Permission

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| must | strong obligation (internal) | You must hand in the form today. |
| have to | obligation (external rule) | You have to show your passport. |
| needn't / don't have to | no necessity | You needn't bring money. |
| mustn't | prohibition | You mustn't park here. |
| can / could | permission | Can I leave early? |
| may / might | formal permission / possibility | You may use a dictionary. |
| should / ought to | advice / mild obligation | You should practise every day. |

---

## Modals 2 — Deduction and Speculation

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| must + inf | near certainty (positive) | She must be tired. |
| can't + inf | near certainty (negative) | That can't be right. |
| might / could + inf | possibility | He might know the answer. |
| must have + pp | past certainty | They must have left already. |
| can't have + pp | past impossibility | She can't have said that. |
| might have + pp | past possibility | He might have forgotten. |

## Conditionals

### Zero Conditional — general truth
`If + present simple, present simple`
- If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.

### First Conditional — real future possibility
`If + present simple, will + infinitive`
- If it rains, I will stay at home.

### Second Conditional — unreal/hypothetical present/future
`If + past simple, would + infinitive`
- If I won the lottery, I would travel the world.

### Third Conditional — unreal past
`If + past perfect, would have + past participle`
- If she had studied harder, she would have passed.

### Mixed Conditional — past cause, present result
`If + past perfect, would + infinitive`
- If I had taken that job, I would be in Paris now.

**unless** = if ... not
- Unless you hurry, we'll miss the train. = If you don't hurry, we'll miss the train.

## Gerunds and Infinitives

### Gerund (verb + -ing) after:
- enjoy / avoid / mind / suggest / finish / consider / keep / practise / admit / deny
- prepositions: I'm good at **cooking**. He left without **saying** goodbye.
- after certain expressions: It's no use / It's worth / Have fun

### Infinitive (to + verb) after:
- want / decide / hope / plan / manage / promise / refuse / seem / afford / agree / offer / tend
- adjectives: It's difficult **to learn** / I'm happy **to help**
- purpose: I went there **to see** my friend.

### Both — with meaning change:
| Verb | + Gerund | + Infinitive |
|------|----------|--------------|
| remember | I remember locking the door (I did it, then remembered) | Remember to lock the door (do it in the future) |
| stop | He stopped smoking (quit) | He stopped to smoke (paused in order to) |
| try | Try eating less sugar (experiment) | Try to eat less sugar (attempt) |
| forget | I'll never forget meeting her (a memory) | I forgot to call you (failed to do it) |

## Reported Speech

| Direct | Reported |
|--------|----------|
| "I am tired" | She said she **was** tired. |
| "I have finished" | He said he **had finished**. |
| "I will call" | She said she **would call**. |
| "I can help" | He said he **could** help. |
| "I went" | She said she **had gone**. |

**Reporting verbs** (vary by structure):
- + that-clause: say, tell (sb), admit, explain, claim, deny, insist
- + to-infinitive: agree, refuse, offer, promise, decide
- + -ing: admit, deny, suggest, recommend

## Passive Voice

**Form:** subject + be (correct tense) + past participle

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Present Simple | They make cars here. | Cars are made here. |
| Past Simple | She wrote the report. | The report was written by her. |
| Present Perfect | Someone has stolen my bike. | My bike has been stolen. |
| Future | They will announce the results. | The results will be announced. |
| Modal | They must test the product. | The product must be tested. |

> 💡 *Use passive when:* the agent is unknown, unimportant, or obvious; in formal/academic writing.

## Relative Clauses

### Defining (no commas — essential information)
- The man **who** called you is waiting outside.
- The book **that/which** I lost was very expensive.

### Non-defining (with commas — extra information)
- My sister, **who** lives in London, is visiting next week.
- The meeting, **which** lasted 3 hours, was exhausting.

> ⚠️ In non-defining clauses: **never use "that"**; the relative pronoun cannot be omitted.

## Future Forms

| Form | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| will + inf | prediction / spontaneous decision | I think it **will** snow. |
| going to + inf | intention / evidence-based prediction | I **am going to** travel next year. |
| Present Continuous | fixed future arrangement | I **am meeting** her at 5. |
| Present Simple | timetabled/scheduled future | The train **leaves** at 9. |
| Future Perfect | action completed before a point in future | By June, I **will have finished**. |
| Future Continuous | ongoing action at a future point | This time tomorrow, I **will be flying**. |

---

## Wish / If Only / It's Time

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| wish + past simple | desire to change present | I wish I **spoke** better English. |
| wish + past perfect | regret about the past | I wish I **had studied** harder. |
| wish + would | desire for change (others' behaviour) | I wish you **would** stop talking. |
| If only | stronger form of wish | If only I **had more** time! |
| It's time + past simple | mild criticism / suggestion | It's time you **went** to bed. |

---

## Participle Clauses

Replace a full clause to make writing more concise.

- **Active:** Walking down the street, I saw an old friend. (= While I was walking...)
- **Passive:** Written in 1950, the novel is still popular. (= Although it was written...)
- **Perfect:** Having finished the exam, she felt relieved. (= After she had finished...)

---

## Emphasis — Cleft Sentences

Used to highlight a particular element.

- **What** I love most is the atmosphere. (= I love the atmosphere most.)
- **It was** Maria **who** found the solution. (= Maria found it.)
- **The thing that** surprised me **was** how cold it was.

---

## used to / would

| Structure | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| used to + inf | past habit OR past state (no longer true) | I **used to** play tennis every weekend. |
| would + inf | past habit ONLY (not states) | We **would** go camping every summer. |

> ⚠️ **would** cannot be used with state verbs: ✗ "I would believe in Santa." ✓ "I used to believe in Santa."

---

## Causative have/get

Express that someone else does something for you.

- **have + object + past participle:** I had my hair cut.
- **get + object + past participle:** She got her car repaired.

Both are interchangeable in most contexts; **get** is slightly more informal.
