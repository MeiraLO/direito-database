# Exam Strategies
> IELTS — Band Descriptors · Time Management · Common Pitfalls

## IELTS Band Descriptors — What Examiners Look For

### Writing
| Criterion | What it means |
|-----------|--------------|
| Task Achievement (T2) / Task Response (T1) | Did you answer the question fully? Is your position clear? |
| Coherence & Cohesion | Is the text well-organised? Do ideas flow logically? |
| Lexical Resource | Variety and accuracy of vocabulary; appropriacy of register |
| Grammatical Range & Accuracy | Variety of structures; how many errors; do errors affect meaning? |

### Speaking
| Criterion | What it means |
|-----------|--------------|
| Fluency & Coherence | Speaks at length without unnatural pausing; ideas connected logically |
| Lexical Resource | Uses a range of vocabulary naturally; uses less common words |
| Grammatical Range & Accuracy | Uses complex structures; errors are rare and don't impede communication |
| Pronunciation | Easy to understand; some features of L1 accent are fine |

> 💡 **Each criterion is worth 25%.** Vocabulary and Grammar together = 50% of your score.

## Band Score Guide (Quick Reference)

| Band | Description |
|------|-------------|
| 9 | Expert user — fully operational command |
| 8 | Very good user — only occasional inaccuracies |
| 7 | Good user — handles complex language, some inaccuracies |
| **6** | **Competent user — generally effective despite some errors** |
| 5 | Modest user — partial command, frequent errors |
| 4 | Limited user — basic competence, frequent breakdowns |

> 💡 Cambridge First (B2) corresponds roughly to IELTS Band **5.5–6.5**

## Time Management

### Academic Writing (60 min total)
| Task | Recommended Time |
|------|----------------|
| Task 2 (essay) | 40 min |
| Task 1 (visual description) | 20 min |
| Checking both tasks | Last 2 min |

> ⚠️ Always do Task 2 FIRST — it is worth twice the marks.

### Academic Reading (60 min)
| Activity | Time |
|----------|------|
| Passage 1 (+ questions) | ~17 min |
| Passage 2 (+ questions) | ~20 min |
| Passage 3 (+ questions) | ~23 min |

> ⚠️ Don't spend more than 2 minutes on any single question — move on and return.

### Listening (30 min audio + 10 min transfer)
- Use every second of preparation time between sections
- Use the 10 min transfer time to check spelling and grammar

### Speaking (11–14 min)
- Part 1: Be conversational and warm
- Part 2: Structure your answer, use all 2 minutes
- Part 3: Develop, justify, speculate — don't give short answers

## Vocabulary Strategy — How to Get Band 7+

### Avoid very basic words — upgrade them:

| Basic | Upgraded |
|-------|---------|
| big | significant / substantial / considerable / vast |
| small | minor / marginal / negligible / modest |
| good | beneficial / advantageous / valuable / positive |
| bad | detrimental / harmful / damaging / adverse |
| important | crucial / vital / significant / fundamental |
| many | a considerable number of / numerous / a wide range of |
| show | demonstrate / illustrate / indicate / reveal |
| say | claim / argue / suggest / state / maintain / assert |
| think | believe / consider / argue / contend |
| get | obtain / acquire / achieve / receive |
| use | utilise / employ / apply / implement |

### Paraphrase the question — don't copy it word for word
- "People nowadays depend too much on technology."
- → "Modern society has become increasingly reliant on technological devices."
- → "There is a growing dependency on digital tools in contemporary life."

## Grammar Strategy — How to Demonstrate Range

Use a variety of structures in your writing and speaking:

- **Passive:** *The report was published in 2023.*
- **Conditional:** *If governments invested more in education, outcomes would improve significantly.*
- **Relative clause:** *The policy, which was introduced in 2020, has had mixed results.*
- **Participle clause:** *Having considered both sides, I believe the benefits outweigh the drawbacks.*
- **Cleft sentence:** *It is the lack of funding that remains the core issue.*
- **Nominal clause:** *What many people fail to realise is that...*
- **Inversion (for formal emphasis):** *Not only does this affect... but it also...*

## Common Mistakes — IELTS Candidates

### Writing
- ❌ Not answering all parts of the question
- ❌ Writing under the word limit (fewer than 250 words for Task 2)
- ❌ Repeating the same vocabulary throughout
- ❌ Using informal language (contractions, slang)
- ❌ Stating opinions without explaining or supporting them
- ❌ Copying chunks of the question into the essay
- ❌ One idea = one paragraph (develop each point fully)

### Speaking
- ❌ Giving one-word or one-sentence answers
- ❌ Using only simple grammar (only present simple, only "I think")
- ❌ Asking the examiner to repeat the question too often
- ❌ Memorised scripted answers (examiners can tell and will change topic)
- ❌ Stopping completely when vocabulary is unknown (paraphrase instead)

### Reading
- ❌ Spending too long on one question
- ❌ Using general knowledge instead of the text
- ❌ Confusing NOT GIVEN with FALSE

### Listening
- ❌ Panicking when a word is missed
- ❌ Not spelling carefully on the answer sheet
- ❌ Writing the first thing heard instead of the final answer given

## Useful Paraphrase Patterns

### Reporting what others believe (academic writing):
- "Many people argue that..." / "It is widely believed that..."
- "Some experts suggest that..." / "Research indicates that..."
- "Critics of this view claim that..." / "Proponents argue that..."

### Hedging your own claims (avoiding absolutes):
- "This may partly explain..." / "It seems likely that..."
- "In most cases..." / "Under certain circumstances..."
- "There is some evidence to suggest..."

### Conceding and countering:
- "While X has some merit, it is important to consider Y."
- "Admittedly, there are benefits to X; however, the drawbacks are more significant."
- "Although this argument is persuasive, it overlooks the fact that..."

## Register Checker

Before submitting any IELTS writing, ask yourself:

- ✅ Have I avoided contractions? (it's → it is / don't → do not)
- ✅ Have I avoided colloquial expressions? (loads of → a significant number of)
- ✅ Have I used a range of vocabulary? (not repeating the same word in every sentence)
- ✅ Have I varied my sentence structures?
- ✅ Have I answered the exact question asked?
- ✅ Have I checked for subject-verb agreement?
- ✅ Have I used articles correctly? (a/an/the)
- ✅ Is my spelling consistent (British or American — not mixed)?
