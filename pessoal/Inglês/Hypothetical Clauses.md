## 1. The four core conditionals

|Type|Structure|Use|Example|
|---|---|---|---|
|Zero|if + present, present|general truths, facts, laws|_If a contract lacks consideration, it is unenforceable._|
|First|if + present, will + infinitive|real/likely future situation|_If the appeal is filed late, the court will reject it._|
|Second|if + past simple, would + infinitive|unreal/unlikely present or future|_If the law were reformed, fewer cases would reach trial._|
|Third|if + past perfect, would have + past participle|unreal past — didn't happen|_If the deadline had been extended, the claim would have succeeded._|

## 2. Mixed conditionals

**Past condition → present result:** _If the legislature had passed the bill (past, unreal), neurorights would already be protected today (present result)._

**Present condition → past result:** _If she were more experienced (present, unreal), she would have won the case last year._

Mixed conditionals are a strong marker of C1/C2 control — examiners notice them. Use when the timeframes of condition and result genuinely differ.

## 3. Formal inversion (no "if")

At C1+, inversion is expected in formal/academic writing as an alternative to _if_:

|Standard|Inverted|
|---|---|
|If the court were to rule against the plaintiff...|**Were** the court to rule against the plaintiff...|
|If the evidence had been disclosed earlier...|**Had** the evidence been disclosed earlier...|
|If I should have any doubts...|**Should** I have any doubts...|

Only _were_, _had_, and _should_ invert this way — not present-tense "if" clauses generally.

## 4. Other ways to express hypothetical meaning

- **Unless** = if...not: _Unless the tribunal intervenes, the ruling stands._ (Do not combine with a negative: ❌ _Unless the tribunal doesn't intervene_)
- **Provided/providing (that), as long as, on condition that** — formal equivalents of "if," common in legal English: _The merger may proceed provided that shareholder approval is obtained._
- **Suppose/supposing, what if** — informal, often used to open a discussion or hypothesis: _Suppose the treaty were ratified — what would change?_
- **But for** + noun phrase (very formal, common in legal writing): _But for his intervention, the case would have been dismissed._ (= If it hadn't been for)
- **Otherwise** — implies a hypothetical alternative from context: _She filed on time; otherwise, the claim would have been barred._
- **If only / I wish** — hypothetical regret or desire, follows same tense-backshift logic as second/third conditional: _I wish the ruling were clearer._ / _If only they had consulted the committee._

## 5. I wish / if only — tense rules

|Time reference|Structure|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Present unreal|wish + past simple|_I wish I understood this doctrine better._|
|Past unreal (regret)|wish + past perfect|_I wish I had read the judgment before the exam._|
|Complaint about others/situation|wish + would|_I wish the professor would clarify the syllabus._|

## 6. Common errors to watch for

- ❌ _If I would have known_ → **If I had known** (don't use "would" in the if-clause of a third conditional — this is a very frequent L1-interference error)
- ❌ _If the law will change_ → **If the law changes** (no "will" in the if-clause of a first conditional)
- ❌ Mismatched tense pairing: _If she was more careful, she will pass_ → decide on one conditional type and keep both clauses consistent
- ❌ Overusing basic "if" when the context calls for formal alternatives (_provided that_, inversion) — in formal writing tasks, variety is rewarded

## 7. Quick self-test

Correct or complete:

1. If the bill would pass, it will take effect in January.
2. ___ the court find in favour of the plaintiff, damages would be awarded.
3. I wish I ___ (know) the answer during the interview yesterday.
4. ___ for the new evidence, the appeal would have failed.
5. Rewrite formally: "If the parties don't agree, the case goes to arbitration."

1. If the bill passes, it will take effect in January.
2. Were the court to find in favour of the plaintiff, damages would be awarded.
3. I wish I had known the answer during the interview yesterday.
4. But for the new evidence, the appeal would have failed.
5. Unless the parties agree, the case goes to arbitration. / Should the parties fail to agree, the case will go to arbitration.
