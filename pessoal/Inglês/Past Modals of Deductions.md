We use past modals of deduction to **speculate about the past** — to say how certain we are that something happened, based on evidence or logic, rather than stating it as a known fact.

**Structure:** `modal + have + past participle`

---

## Certainty — must have

**Use:** when you're almost certain something happened, based on strong evidence.

- She **must have left** already — her coat is gone.
- The registry **must have received** the petition; they confirmed it by phone.

---

## Possibility — could have / might have / may have

**Use:** when something is a possible explanation, but you're not sure.

- He **could have missed** the deadline — I'm not certain.
- They **might have sent** the documents to the wrong office.
- The delay **may have been caused** by the holiday closures.

> `could have`, `might have`, and `may have` are largely interchangeable here, though `might have` is the most common in everyday speech.

---

## Impossibility — can't have / couldn't have

**Use:** when you're almost certain something did **not** happen — the negative equivalent of `must have`.

- She **can't have forgotten** the hearing — she mentioned it yesterday.
- He **couldn't have signed** the document; he was out of the country.

> ⚠️ Don't use `mustn't have` for this meaning — it's not standard for past deduction. Use **can't have** or **couldn't have** instead.

---

## Quick Reference Table

|Modal|Degree of certainty|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**must have**|strong positive certainty|The court **must have notified** them — it's standard procedure.|
|**could have**|possible|The notary **could have made** an error.|
|**might have**|possible|She **might have missed** the notification.|
|**may have**|possible (slightly more formal)|The claim **may have been filed** late.|
|**can't have**|strong negative certainty|He **can't have read** the whole contract that fast.|
|**couldn't have**|strong negative certainty|They **couldn't have known** about the change in time.|

---

## Common Errors to Watch For

- **Don't drop `have`**: ~~She must left already~~ → _She **must have left** already._
- **Don't use `must not have`** for impossibility — use **can't have / couldn't have**.
- **Past participle, not base form**: ~~could have leave~~ → _could have **left**._
- **Pronunciation note**: in speech, `have` after a modal is often reduced to /əv/ (sounds like "must've," "could've") — but always write it out fully in formal writing.
