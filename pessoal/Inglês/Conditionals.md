
Conditionals describe the relationship between a condition (the `if`-clause) and its result (the main clause). English has five core types: zero, first, second, third, and mixed.

---

## Zero Conditional

**Use:** general truths, facts, scientific laws, habitual results — things that are _always_ true when the condition is met.

**Structure:** `If + present simple, ... present simple`

- If you **heat** water to 100°C, it **boils**.
- If a contract **lacks** a valid signature, it **is** unenforceable.

> Note: `if` and `when` are often interchangeable here, since there's no uncertainty involved.

---

## First Conditional

**Use:** real and possible future situations — a condition that could realistically happen, with a likely result.

**Structure:** `If + present simple, ... will + base verb`

- If the registry **rejects** the petition, we **will file** an appeal.
- If she **submits** the documents today, the process **will move** faster.

> Modal alternatives to `will`: _can, may, might, should_ — used when the result is a possibility, permission, or recommendation rather than a certainty.
> 
> - If the fee waiver is approved, you **may proceed** without payment.

---

## Second Conditional

**Use:** hypothetical or unlikely situations in the present/future — imagined, not real.

**Structure:** `If + past simple, ... would + base verb`

- If I **had** more time, I **would review** the contract more carefully.
- If the law **were** different, this case **would be** much simpler.

> Use **were** instead of _was_ for all subjects in formal/written English (_"If I were you..."_), even though _was_ is common in casual speech.

---

## Third Conditional

**Use:** hypothetical situations in the **past** — things that didn't happen, and their imagined (also past) result. Often used to express regret or criticism. 

**Structure:** `If + past perfect, ... would have + past participle`

- If the client **had submitted** the documents on time, the judge **would have granted** the request.
- If we **had known** about the deadline, we **would have acted** sooner.

---

## Mixed Conditionals

**Use:** when the time in the `if`-clause and the time in the result clause don't match — a past condition with a present result, or a present condition with a past result.

### Type 1 — Past condition → Present result

**Structure:** `If + past perfect, ... would + base verb`

- If she **had studied** law in Portugal, she **would live** in Coimbra now. _(past unreal condition, present unreal result)_

### Type 2 — Present condition → Past result

**Structure:** `If + past simple, ... would have + past participle`

- If he **weren't** so disorganized, he **would have submitted** the petition on time. _(present unreal condition, past unreal result)_

---

## Quick Reference Table

|Type|If-clause|Main clause|Use|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Zero**|present simple|present simple|general truths|
|**First**|present simple|will + base verb|real future possibility|
|**Second**|past simple|would + base verb|hypothetical present/future|
|**Third**|past perfect|would have + past participle|hypothetical past|
|**Mixed (past→present)**|past perfect|would + base verb|past condition, present result|
|**Mixed (present→past)**|past simple|would have + past participle|present condition, past result|

---

## Common Errors to Watch For

- **Don't mix `will` into the if-clause**: ~~If it will rain, I will stay home~~ → _If it **rains**, I will stay home._
- **Don't use `would` in the if-clause of second/third conditionals**: ~~If I would have more time~~ → _If I **had** more time..._
- **Comma rule**: use a comma when the `if`-clause comes first; no comma when it comes second.
    - _If you submit the form, you will receive confirmation._
    - _You will receive confirmation if you submit the form._
