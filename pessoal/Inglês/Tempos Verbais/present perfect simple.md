# Present Perfect

## 📌 What is the Present Perfect?

The Present Perfect connects the past to the present. It describes actions that **happened in the past but have relevance, results, or effects in the present**. The exact time is NOT mentioned — because it is either unknown or unimportant.

- **ATTENTION:** Present perfect simple can't use action verbs (-ing) (that's present perfect continuous)
---

## 📐 Structure

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Affirmative | Subject + **have/has** + past participle | I have visited three countries. |
| ❌ Negative | Subject + **haven't/hasn't** + past participle | She hasn't finished yet. |
| ❓ Question | **Have/Has** + subject + past participle? | Have you ever eaten sushi? |

---

## 🧠 When to Use the Present Perfect

- Past action with a **result that matters now**.
  > *I have lost my keys.* (I still don't have them — this is my current problem)
  > *She has passed the exam.* (she is qualified now)

- Life **experiences** (without saying when).
  > *I have been to France three times.*
  > *He has never tried sushi.*

- Actions that **just happened**.
  > *She has just arrived.*
  > *The doctor has just reviewed the file.*

- Actions that **started in the past and continue now** (with *since* / *for*).
  > *I have studied law for three years.* (I still study it)
  > *She has lived here since 2020.*

- Actions in an **unfinished time period**.
  > *I haven't had coffee today.* (today is not over)
  > *Has she called you this week?*

---

## ⏰ Key Time Expressions

| Expression | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| already | Something happened sooner than expected | I have already finished the report. |
| yet | Expected action not done (negatives/questions) | Have you eaten yet? / I haven't yet. |
| just | Very recently | He has just left the office. |
| ever | At any time in life (questions) | Have you ever seen that film? |
| never | Not at any time in life | She has never been abroad. |
| since + point | Marks the start of a period | I've known him since 2015. |
| for + duration | Marks how long | They have worked here for 10 years. |
| recently / lately | In the near past | Have you been feeling better lately? |
| so far | Up to this point | So far, we have won every case. |

---

## 🇧🇷 British vs American English

There is an important difference in how the Present Perfect is used:

| | British English | American English |
|---|---|---|
| just / already / yet | Always uses Present Perfect | Often uses Past Simple |
| Example | *"I have just arrived."* | *"I just arrived."* |

> ⚠️ **Note for Cambridge (CAE) and Tilburg University:** always use British English conventions — use Present Perfect with *just*, *already*, and *yet*.

## 💬 Extended Examples

> ✅ *The court has issued its ruling.* (result: it is now official)
> ✅ *I haven't read that legal theory yet.* (I plan to)
> ❓ *Have you ever studied constitutional law?*
> ✅ *She has been a judge for over 20 years.*
> ✅ *We have already submitted the petition.*
> ✅ *He has worked in the public defender's office since he graduated.*
