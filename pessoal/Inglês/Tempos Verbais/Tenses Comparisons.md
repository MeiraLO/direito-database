# Comparisons & Differences

## 🗺️ The Big Picture — Timeline

Understanding when each tense is used becomes much easier when you visualise the timeline:

```
PAST ◄──────────────────────────────────────────────── NOW ────────────────► FUTURE

Past Perfect       Past Simple       Past Continuous       Present Perfect
  (had + pp)        (verb-ed)        (was/were + -ing)      (have/has + pp)
      ●────────────────●                  ▓▓▓▓▓▓                 ●────────────►
  (earlier)         (later)          (in progress)         (result still matters)
```

---

## ⚔️ Comparison 1: Past Simple vs Past Continuous

The key difference: **Past Simple = completed action; Past Continuous = ongoing action at a moment.**

| Past Simple | Past Continuous |
|---|---|
| Completed, finished action | Action in progress at a specific moment |
| Emphasises the event itself | Emphasises duration / background |
| The interrupting action | The action being interrupted |
| *I read the entire book.* | *I was reading the book when she called.* |
| *It rained last night.* | *It was raining when I left the office.* |
| *He made coffee.* | *He was making coffee while I was setting the table.* |

### Classic Pattern: While / When

> *I was walking home (past continuous) **when** it started to rain (past simple).*
> *She was reviewing the case **when** the judge called her.*
> ***While** he was sleeping (past continuous), someone knocked on the door (past simple).*

> 💡 **Rule:** *When* introduces the interrupting event (Past Simple). *While* introduces the ongoing background (Past Continuous).

## ⚔️ Comparison 2: Past Simple vs Present Perfect

This is the most common source of confusion — especially because Brazilian Portuguese often uses one form where English uses two different tenses.

| Past Simple | Present Perfect |
|---|---|
| Specific time is mentioned or implied | Time is unknown, irrelevant, or unfinished |
| The action is purely in the past | The action has a result or effect now |
| Used with: *yesterday, last week, ago, in 2010* | Used with: *already, yet, ever, never, since, for* |
| *I lost my keys yesterday.* | *I have lost my keys.* (I still can't find them) |
| *She studied in Tilburg last semester.* | *She has studied in the Netherlands.* (life experience) |
| *Did you eat dinner?* | *Have you eaten dinner yet?* |
| *He worked there for 10 years.* (he no longer does) | *He has worked there for 10 years.* (he still does) |

### Test Yourself — Which is correct?

| Sentence | Correct Tense | Why? |
|---|---|---|
| I ___ (see) that film three times. | **have seen** | Life experience — no specific time. |
| I ___ (see) that film on Friday. | **saw** | Specific time (Friday) given. |
| She ___ (live) here since 2020. | **has lived** | Ongoing — she still lives here. |
| She ___ (live) here in 2020. | **lived** | Specific year — completed period. |
| ___ you ever (be) to Japan? | **Have you ever been** | Life experience question. |

## ⚔️ Comparison 3: Present Perfect vs Past Perfect

Both use a past participle. The key difference is the **reference point**.

| Present Perfect | Past Perfect |
|---|---|
| Reference point = **NOW** (the present) | Reference point = **A PAST MOMENT** |
| Result/effect connects to the present | Action completed before another past event |
| *have / has* + past participle | *had* + past participle |
| *She has submitted the documents.* (they're in now) | *She had submitted the documents before the deadline passed.* |
| *I have never seen this before.* (up to now) | *I had never seen anything like it before that day.* |
| *Has he called?* (up to now) | *Had he called before you left?* |

---

## ⚔️ Comparison 4: Past Simple vs Past Perfect

Both are fully in the past. The difference is about **which event happened first**.

| Earlier Event (Past Perfect) | Later Event (Past Simple) |
|---|---|
| She had already left | when I arrived. |
| He hadn't eaten anything | so he ordered pizza. |
| By the time the rescue team arrived, | the patient had lost consciousness. |
| The lawyer had reviewed all the files | before the trial began. |

> ⚠️ **Note:** If *before* or *after* makes the order clear, you can sometimes use Past Simple for both events. But Past Perfect is preferred in formal, academic, and legal writing.

## 📊 Master Comparison Table

| | Past Simple | Past Continuous | Present Perfect | Past Perfect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Time reference** | Specific past time | Specific past moment | Connected to now | Before another past event |
| **Duration focus** | No | Yes | With for/since | No |
| **Result in present** | No | No | Yes | No |
| **Auxiliary** | did (for ?/−) | was/were | have/has | had |
| **Main verb form** | Verb-ed / irregular | Verb-ing | Past participle | Past participle |
| **Key words** | yesterday, ago, last, in 2010 | while, when, at that time, all day | already, yet, ever, for, since | by the time, before, after, already |

## 🔄 The Same Story — 4 Tenses

Here is the same event described using all four tenses. Notice how the meaning shifts:

| Tense | Sentence | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| **Past Simple** | The nurse treated the patient. | A finished event. Basic fact. Nothing more. |
| **Past Continuous** | The nurse was treating the patient when the doctor arrived. | The treatment was in progress — it provides the background. |
| **Present Perfect** | The nurse has treated the patient. | The treatment is done and the result matters now (the patient is better). |
| **Past Perfect** | By the time the doctor arrived, the nurse had already treated the patient. | The treatment was completed before the doctor's arrival — the nurse acted first. |

## 💡 Quick Decision Guide

Ask yourself these questions to choose the right tense:

| Question | If YES → Use |
|---|---|
| Is the time specific and mentioned? | **Past Simple** |
| Was the action in progress at a past moment? | **Past Continuous** |
| Does the past action affect the present? / Is the time irrelevant? | **Present Perfect** |
| Did this happen before another past event? | **Past Perfect** |
| Is there a background + interruption? | **Past Continuous + Past Simple** |
| Are two past events completed and you need to show which came first? | **Past Perfect + Past Simple** |
