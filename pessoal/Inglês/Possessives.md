
## 1. Forms

| Form                  | Use                                               | Example                                                                                                                          |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 's                    | singular nouns, irregular plurals, names          | _the client's file_, _the children's rights_, _James's argument_                                                                 |
| s'                    | regular plural nouns and names that end with an S | _the lawyers' offices_, _the students' essays_                                                                                   |
| of + noun             | inanimate/abstract nouns, formal register         | _the terms of the contract_ (not _the contract's terms_, though this is now acceptable in formal writing) (don't use apostrophe) |
| possessive pronouns   | mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs         | _This argument is stronger than theirs._                                                                                         |
| possessive adjectives | my, your, his, her, its, our, their               | _their obligation to disclose_                                                                                                   |

## 2. Key rules

**Singular nouns ending in -s:** add 's (preferred in formal/academic style) — _the witness's testimony_, _Charles's statement_. Some style guides accept the s'-only form (_Charles' statement_) — <u>be consistent within a document.</u>

**Regular plurals:** apostrophe only after the s — _the parties' rights_, _the states' constitutions_.

**Irregular plurals:** treat as singular, add 's — _the people's will_, _the media's coverage_.

**Compound nouns / joint possession:**

- Joint ownership → mark only the last noun: _João and Maria's apartment_ (they own it together)
- Separate ownership → mark both: _João's and Maria's arguments_ (each made their own argument)

**Possessive + gerund (formal register):** _the defendant's failing to appear_ (formal) vs _the defendant failing to appear_ (informal, more common in speech). Legal and academic writing favours the possessive form.

## 3. its vs it's

- **its** = possessive adjective, no apostrophe — _the court delivered its ruling_
- **it's** = it is / it has — _it's important to note..._ This is the single most common error even at advanced levels — always double-check before submitting.

## 4. When there is NO apostrophe

**Possessive pronouns** — mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs

- _This argument is stronger than hers._
- _The decision was theirs to make._

**Possessive adjectives** — my, your, his, her, its, our, their

- _its ruling_, _their obligation_
- **its** (possessive adjective) never takes an apostrophe — see Section 3 above.

**Descriptive/attributive plurals that aren't true possession** — when a plural noun functions more like an adjective labelling a category rather than showing ownership, especially in institutional names:

- _Veterans Affairs_, _Citizens Advice_, _a teachers college_
- Test: is anything actually "belonging to" the noun, or is it just naming a type? Usage varies (_farmers market_ vs _farmers' market_) — pick one convention and stay consistent within a document.

**Some time/measurement expressions in informal style** — _three weeks notice_ alongside the more standard _three weeks' notice_. For formal/legal and exam writing, keep the apostrophe here (_two years' imprisonment_, _a day's notice_) — this is the safer choice.

**Reliable rule:** apostrophes mark possession on **nouns**. Pronouns and adjectives already show possession through their form, so they never take one.

## 5. 's vs of-construction

Use **'s** for:

- people, animals, countries, organisations as agents: _Brazil's legislation_, _the committee's decision_
- time expressions: _today's hearing_, _a week's notice_

Use **of** for:

- inanimate objects and abstract concepts, especially in formal/legal writing: _the scope of the law_, _the burden of proof_
- when the possessor is a long phrase: _the decision of the judge who presided over the case_ (avoid an awkward long 's chain)

## 6. Double possessives

_a friend of my sister's_ — used to indicate one of several, common in speech; in formal writing prefer _one of my sister's friends_.

## 7. Common errors to watch for (based on your recurring patterns)

- ❌ _this evidences_ → this is not possessive at all; check you're not confusing plural -s with possessive -'s (e.g. _the courts decision_ → **the court's decision**)
- ❌ _the childrens' toys_ → children is already plural; the possessive is **children's**
- ❌ _its' obligations_ → **its'** does not exist; it's either **its** or **it's**
- ❌ Dropping the apostrophe entirely in fast writing under exam time pressure — proofread possessives specifically in your last minute of checking
