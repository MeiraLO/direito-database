# Pronouns — C1 Advanced (CAE) Guide

## What Is a Pronoun?
A pronoun replaces a noun to avoid repetition. At C1, the focus is on **relative pronouns** (defining vs. non-defining clauses), **indefinite pronouns** with subject-verb agreement, and the reflexive/emphatic distinction.

## 1. Personal Pronouns

| Subject | Object |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| you | you |
| he / she / it | him / her / it |
| we | us |
| they | them |

- *She* submitted the brief. → The judge thanked *her*.

## 2. Possessive Adjectives vs. Possessive Pronouns

| Possessive Adjective (+ noun) | Possessive Pronoun (stands alone) |
|---|---|
| my | mine |
| your | yours |
| his / her / its | his / hers / — |
| our | ours |
| their | theirs |

- *This is my file.* → *This file is mine.*
- ⚠️ *Its* (possessive) vs *it's* (it is) — a classic written error even among native speakers.

## 3. Reflexive & Emphatic Pronouns
Same forms (*myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves*), two functions:

- **Reflexive** — the action returns to the subject: *He hurt himself during the hearing.*
- **Emphatic** — adds emphasis, often movable: *The judge herself raised the objection.* / *The judge raised the objection herself.*

> C1 tip: Don't use a reflexive pronoun in place of a simple object pronoun: ❌ ~~Please contact myself for details.~~ → ✅ **Please contact me for details.**

## 4. Relative Pronouns — Defining vs. Non-Defining Clauses
One of the highest-value C1 points, tested throughout Use of English and Writing.

| Pronoun | Refers to | Use |
|---|---|---|
| who | people (subject) | the lawyer *who* argued the case |
| whom | people (object, formal) | the witness *whom* they questioned |
| whose | possession | the client *whose* case was dismissed |
| which | things | the ruling *which* set a precedent |
| that | people/things (defining only) | the law *that* changed everything |
| where | places | the court *where* the trial was held |
| when | time | the year *when* the law passed |

**Defining clauses** (no commas — essential information)
- The witness *who* testified yesterday was credible.
- *That* can replace *who/which*: The witness *that* testified...
- The pronoun can be **omitted** if it's the object of the clause: *The case (that) she mentioned was dismissed.*

**Non-defining clauses** (commas — extra, non-essential information)
- My professor, *who* specializes in civil law, recommended this article.
- ⚠️ *That* is **never** used here: ❌ ~~My professor, that specializes...~~
- The pronoun can **never** be omitted here.

## 5. Demonstrative Pronouns
*this, that, these, those* — point to something specific.
- *This* argument is stronger than *that* one.
- *These* precedents support *those* conclusions.

## 6. Indefinite Pronouns
*someone, anyone, no one, everybody, something, anything, nothing, each, either, neither, none, all, both*

- Compound forms (*-one, -body, -thing*) take a **singular** verb: *Everyone agrees* (not *agree*).
- *None* can take a singular or plural verb depending on formality: *None of the witnesses was/were present.*
- *Each* + singular verb: *Each party has* the right to appeal.

## 7. Reciprocal Pronouns
*each other* (typically two) vs *one another* (typically three or more, though usage overlaps).
- The two parties accused *each other* of bad faith.
- The committee members consulted *one another* before voting.

## Common Mistakes

**General**
- Using *that* in a non-defining (comma) clause.
- Confusing *its* and *it's*.
- Overusing reflexive pronouns as plain objects (*myself* instead of *me*).

**For Portuguese speakers**
- Portuguese is a pro-drop language (subject pronouns are often omitted, since verb endings already show the subject), which leads to dropping required English subject pronouns or the dummy subject *it/there*: ❌ "Is raining" → ✅ **It is raining**; ❌ "Has many problems with this law" → ✅ **There are many problems with this law.**
- *"Que"* covers both *who* and *which/that* in Portuguese, so learners often default to *that*/*which* for people: ❌ "the lawyer which argued" → ✅ **the lawyer who argued**.

## Quick Practice
1. The contract, ___ was signed last week, is now void. (which / that)
2. ___ raining heavily when the trial began. (It was / Was)
3. The defendant blamed ___ for the outcome. (himself / hisself)
4. This is not my copy — ___ is on the desk. (mine / my)
5. The two firms have been competing with ___ for years. (each other / themselves)

**Answers:** 1. which 2. It was 3. himself 4. mine 5. each other
