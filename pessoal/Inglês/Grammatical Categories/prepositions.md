# Prepositions — C1 Advanced (CAE) Guide

## What Is a Preposition?
A preposition shows the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another part of the sentence (time, place, direction, manner). At C1, the real challenge is **dependent prepositions** — fixed combinations with verbs, adjectives, and nouns — heavily tested in Use of English Part 2 (open cloze).

## 1. Prepositions of Time

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| at | precise times, night | *at* 9 a.m., *at* night |
| on | days, dates | *on* Monday, *on* 3 July |
| in | months, years, periods | *in* August, *in* 2024, *in* the morning |
| since | starting point | working there *since* 2020 |
| for | duration | *for* three years |
| during | within a period | *during* the trial |
| by | deadline | submit *by* Friday |
| within | a limit | a response *within* 30 days |
| until/till | up to a point | open *until* 6 p.m. |

## 2. Prepositions of Place & Movement

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| in | enclosed space | *in* the courtroom |
| on | surface | *on* the desk |
| at | point/location | *at* the entrance |
| into | movement inward | walked *into* the office |
| onto | movement onto a surface | jumped *onto* the table |
| through | movement across/inside | *through* the corridor |
| across | side to side | *across* the street |
| along | following a line | *along* the river |
| over / under | above / below | *over* the bridge, *under* the table |
| between / among | two / more than two | *between* the parties, *among* the witnesses |

## 3. Dependent Prepositions (the C1 essential)

### Verb + Preposition

| Verb | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| depend | on | The outcome *depends on* the evidence. |
| consist | of | The committee *consists of* five members. |
| succeed | in | She *succeeded in* passing the exam. |
| apologize | for | He *apologized for* the delay. |
| accuse (sb) | of | They *accused* him *of* fraud. |
| congratulate (sb) | on | I *congratulated* her *on* the result. |
| insist | on | The lawyer *insisted on* a retrial. |
| deal | with | The court will *deal with* the appeal. |
| comply | with | The firm must *comply with* the regulation. |
| benefit | from | Tenants *benefit from* the new law. |
| object | to | The defense *objected to* the question. |
| refer | to | The clause *refers to* Article 32. |

### Adjective + Preposition

| Adjective | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| interested | in | *interested in* civil law |
| keen | on | *keen on* the new internship |
| famous | for | *famous for* his rulings |
| capable | of | *capable of* handling the case |
| responsible | for | *responsible for* the breach |
| married | to | *married to* a colleague |
| similar | to | *similar to* the previous case |
| aware | of | *aware of* the deadline |
| satisfied | with | *satisfied with* the outcome |
| good / bad | at | *good at* legal writing |

### Noun + Preposition

| Noun | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| reason | for | the *reason for* the delay |
| solution | to | a *solution to* the dispute |
| increase / decrease | in | an *increase in* filings |
| access | to | *access to* the records |
| relationship | between / with | the *relationship between* the parties |
| demand | for | *demand for* reform |

## 4. Prepositions at the End of a Sentence
Common and natural in neutral/informal English, especially with relative clauses and questions.

- Neutral: *That's the case I was telling you about.*
- Formal: *That is the case about which I was telling you.*

CAE Writing tasks in a formal register (reports, formal letters) benefit from the "preposition before the relative pronoun" structure; everyday English and most exam tasks accept the end-position naturally.

## Common Mistakes

**General**
- Treating *in time* and *on time* as interchangeable — they're not (*on time* = punctual; *in time* = before it's too late).
- Forgetting a verb needs no preposition at all: ~~discuss about~~ → **discuss**.

**For Portuguese speakers**
- *depender de* → ❌ "depend of" → ✅ **depend on**
- *casado com* → ❌ "married with" → ✅ **married to**
- *bom em* → ❌ "good in" → ✅ **good at**
- Portuguese verbs like *explicar* and *ouvir* take a direct object, so the preposition gets dropped in English: ❌ "explain me the rule" → ✅ **explain the rule to me**; ❌ "listen me" → ✅ **listen to me**.

3. He was accused ___ withholding evidence.
