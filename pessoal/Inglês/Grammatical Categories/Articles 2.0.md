# Articles — A, AN, THE, and Zero Article

### English Grammar — Document 11

---

## 📌 What is an Article?

An article is a word placed **before a noun** to indicate whether the noun is specific or general, known or unknown, countable or uncountable.

English has three article situations:

|Article|Name|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**a / an**|Indefinite article|_She is **a** lawyer._|
|**the**|Definite article|_**The** lawyer arrived late._|
|_(nothing)_|Zero article|_**Lawyers** are essential in society._|

---

## 🚨 Articles vs Prepositions — An Important Distinction

Before anything else, it is crucial to understand that **articles are NOT prepositions**, even though both appear before nouns.

||Article|Preposition|
|---|---|---|
|**What it does**|Identifies the noun (specific/general)|Shows relationship between elements (place, time, cause...)|
|**Examples**|a, an, the|of, in, on, at, by, for, with...|
|**Followed by**|A noun directly|A noun, pronoun, or noun phrase|
|**Can be removed?**|Sometimes (zero article)|No — the sentence loses meaning|

Compare:

> _She studied **the** law._ → **the** is an article identifying which law _She studied **in** the Netherlands._ → **in** is a preposition showing place; **the** is still the article before Netherlands

A word like **"of"** is a **preposition** — it shows possession, quantity, or relationship:

> _A piece **of** evidence._ → **of** connects "piece" to "evidence" — it is not identifying the noun, it is relating two nouns _The role **of** the judge._ → **of** shows relationship — **the** before "judge" is the article

> 💡 **Key test:** Can you replace the word with _a_, _an_, or _the_? If yes, it is an article. If not, it is a preposition.
> 
> - _She is **a** lawyer._ → **a** can become **the** → article ✅
> - _A piece **of** advice._ → **of** cannot become _the_ or _a_ → preposition ✅

## ─────────────────────────────────────────

## PART 1 — A / AN (The Indefinite Article)

## ─────────────────────────────────────────

## 📌 What does A / AN mean?

**A** and **an** signal that the noun is **not specific** — you are referring to any one of something, or introducing it for the first time.

> _She is **a** lawyer._ → any lawyer; we haven't specified which one _He found **an** error in the document._ → one error, introduced for the first time

---

## A or AN — Which One?

The choice depends entirely on the **sound** that follows — not the spelling.

|Use|Rule|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|**a**|Before a **consonant sound**|**a** book, **a** judge, **a** university*, **a** European*|
|**an**|Before a **vowel sound**|**an** apple, **an** idea, **an** honest man*, **an** hour*|

> ⚠️ **Sound, not spelling — the most common mistake:**
> 
> - _university_ starts with **u** but sounds like **"you"** (consonant sound) → **a** university ✅
> - _hour_ starts with **h** but the h is silent → sounds like **"our"** (vowel sound) → **an** hour ✅
> - _honest_ — silent h → **an** honest person ✅
> - _European_ sounds like **"you"** → **a** European country ✅
> - _unique_ sounds like **"you"** → **a** unique case ✅

### More examples of the sound rule

|Word|Sounds like|Correct form|
|---|---|---|
|umbrella|/ʌ/ — vowel|**an** umbrella|
|uniform|/juː/ — consonant|**a** uniform|
|uncle|/ʌ/ — vowel|**an** uncle|
|heir|/eə/ — vowel (h silent)|**an** heir|
|herb (British)|/hɜːb/ — h pronounced|**a** herb|
|herb (American)|/ɜːb/ — h silent|**an** herb|

---

## When to Use A / AN

**1. Introducing something for the first time**

> _I read **a** petition yesterday. **The** petition was about land rights._ _(First mention → a. Second mention → the.)_

**2. Referring to any member of a group — non-specific**

> _She wants to become **a** judge._ (one of many judges — no specific one) _Can I borrow **a** pen?_ (any pen)

**3. Talking about jobs, roles, or identity**

> _He is **a** law student at UFF._ _She works as **a** public defender._ _He became **a** senator in 2019._

**4. In exclamations with "what"**

> _What **a** brilliant argument!_ _What **an** extraordinary case!_

**5. Meaning "one" — quantity**

> _I need **a** minute._ (= one minute) _He didn't say **a** word._

**6. Prices, speeds, and ratios — meaning "per"**

> _The speed limit is 80 km **an** hour._ _She earns R$50 **a** day._ _Take it twice **a** day._

---

## When NOT to Use A / AN

**1. With plural nouns**

> ❌ _She is **a** lawyers._ → ✅ _She is **a** lawyer._ / _They are lawyers._

**2. With uncountable nouns**

> ❌ _She gave me **an** advice._ ✅ _She gave me **some** advice._ / _She gave me **a piece of** advice._

Common uncountable nouns:

> information, advice, evidence, knowledge, research, news, furniture, luggage, progress, traffic, weather, money

**3. With proper nouns**

> ❌ _She lives in **a** Brazil._ → ✅ _She lives in Brazil._

## ─────────────────────────────────────────

## PART 2 — THE (The Definite Article)

## ─────────────────────────────────────────

## 📌 What does THE mean?

**The** signals that both the speaker and listener know **exactly which** thing is being referred to.

> _Pass me **the** file._ → a specific file we both know about _She won **the** case._ → a specific case already in context

**The** is the most common word in the English language — and also one of the most misused by Portuguese speakers, who sometimes add or omit it where English would not.

---

## When to Use THE

**1. Second mention — after introducing with a/an**

> _A witness came forward. **The** witness told police everything._ _She filed **a** petition. **The** petition was accepted._

**2. When there is only one — unique things**

> _**The** sun / **the** moon / **the** internet / **the** Constitution / **the** government_ _**The** president signed the bill._ (there is only one president)

**3. When context makes it obvious which one**

> _She went to **the** kitchen._ (the kitchen of the house) _Can you close **the** door?_ (the door that is open right now) _He called **the** police._ (the institution)

**4. Superlatives — always use THE**

> _It was **the** most complex case she had ever handled._ _He is **the** best lawyer in the firm._

**5. Ordinal numbers**

> _She was **the** first woman to win that award._ _This is **the** third time he has appealed._

**6. With "same", "only", "last"**

> _We reached **the** same conclusion._ _She was **the** only witness._

**7. Rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, deserts**

> _**The** Amazon / **the** Pacific Ocean / **the** Alps / **the** Sahara_

**8. Countries with plural names or containing a common noun**

> _**The** United States / **the** United Kingdom / **the** Netherlands / **the** Dominican Republic_

**9. Newspapers, ships, hotels, organisations**

> _**The** Guardian / **the** Titanic / **the** Ritz / **the** United Nations / **the** Supreme Court_

**10. Musical instruments (when played)**

> _She plays **the** piano. / He learned **the** guitar._

**11. Nationalities used as a group**

> _**The** French / **the** Dutch / **the** Brazilian legal system_

---

## When NOT to Use THE

**1. With proper names — people and most places**

> ❌ _I spoke to **the** Judge Silva._ → ✅ _I spoke to Judge Silva._ ❌ _She lives in **the** Brazil._ → ✅ _She lives in Brazil._

**2. With languages**

> ❌ _She speaks **the** Portuguese._ → ✅ _She speaks Portuguese._

**3. With meals in general**

> ❌ _We had **the** lunch._ → ✅ _We had lunch._ ✅ _But: **The** lunch she prepared was excellent._ (specific meal)

**4. With sports and games**

> ❌ _She plays **the** football._ → ✅ _She plays football._

**5. With academic subjects**

> ❌ _She studies **the** law._ → ✅ _She studies law._ ✅ _But: **The** law she studies is constitutional law._ (specific)

**6. With transport using "by"**

> ❌ _She goes by **the** bus._ → ✅ _She goes by bus._

---

## THE vs No Article — The Tricky Pairs

|With THE (physical place)|Without THE (purpose/function)|
|---|---|
|_She visited him in **the** prison._|_He is in prison._ (= he is imprisoned)|
|_He works in **the** hospital._|_She is in hospital._ 🇬🇧 (= she is a patient)|
|_She went to **the** school to meet the teacher._|_He goes to school every day._ (= he is a student)|
|_**The** church on the corner is beautiful._|_She goes to church on Sundays._ (= religious practice)|

> 💡 This distinction — **building vs institution/activity** — appears frequently in Cambridge exams.

## ─────────────────────────────────────────

## PART 3 — ZERO ARTICLE (No Article)

## ─────────────────────────────────────────

## 📌 What is the Zero Article?

The zero article means using **no article at all**. This is not a mistake — it is a deliberate grammatical choice. It is used when speaking about things **in general**, with proper nouns, and in many fixed expressions.

> ❌ _She studies **the** law at **the** UFF._ ✅ _She studies law at UFF._

---

## When to Use NO Article

**1. Plural countable nouns — general statements**

> _**Lawyers** must pass the bar exam._ (lawyers in general) _**Judges** are appointed in Brazil._

Compare: _**The** lawyers in this case were experienced._ (specific lawyers → use the)

**2. Uncountable nouns — general statements**

> _**Justice** must be served._ _**Evidence** was found at the scene._ _**Knowledge** of the law is not enough._

Compare: _**The** evidence found at the scene was conclusive._ (specific → use the)

**3. Names of people**

> _**Meira** is studying law at UFF._ _**Judge Silva** presided over the case._

**4. Names of most countries, cities, and continents**

> _She lives in **Brazil** / **France** / **Japan**._ _He visited **São Paulo** / **Niterói** / **Paris**._ _They travelled across **South America** / **Europe**._

Exception: _The United States / the Netherlands / the United Kingdom_

**5. Languages**

> _She speaks **Portuguese**, **English**, and some **French**._

**6. Academic subjects**

> _She studies **law**, **history**, and **philosophy**._

**7. Sports and games**

> _She plays **football** and **tennis**._

**8. Meals in general**

> _We had **breakfast** at 7 AM. / **Lunch** is served at noon._

**9. Transport with "by"**

> _She travels by **bus** / by **train** / by **plane** / by **car**._

**10. Fixed expressions**

|Expression|Example|
|---|---|
|at home|She is **at home** today.|
|at work|He is **at work** until 6 PM.|
|in court|She spent the morning **in court**.|
|in bed|He stayed **in bed** all day.|
|on time|The verdict was delivered **on time**.|
|on holiday|They are **on holiday** this week.|
|by mistake|He signed it **by mistake**.|
|on foot|She walked there **on foot**.|
|face to face|They met **face to face**.|
|day by day|**Day by day**, things improved.|

## 📊 Master Comparison Table — All Three Articles

|Sentence|Article|Why|
|---|---|---|
|_I need **a** lawyer._|**a**|Any lawyer — first mention, non-specific|
|_I spoke to **the** lawyer._|**the**|A specific lawyer we both know|
|_**Lawyers** are essential in society._|**zero**|Lawyers in general|
|_She drank **a** coffee._|**a**|One coffee — informal countable use|
|_She drank **the** coffee I made._|**the**|A specific coffee|
|_She doesn't drink **coffee**._|**zero**|Coffee in general — uncountable|
|_He lives in **a** city._|**a**|An unspecified city|
|_He lives in **the** city centre._|**the**|A specific known place|
|_He lives in **São Paulo**._|**zero**|Proper name of a city|
