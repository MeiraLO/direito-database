# Adverbs — C1 Advanced (CAE) Guide

## What Is an Adverb?
An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb, or a whole sentence. At C1, precision in **position** and **register** matters more than basic formation — and linking adverbs are a major source of Use of English errors.

## 1. Types of Adverbs

| Type | Examples | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Manner | carefully, quickly, well | She argued the case *skillfully*. |
| Place | here, there, abroad | The hearing took place *there*. |
| Time | now, yesterday, soon | The verdict will be announced *soon*. |
| Frequency | always, often, rarely, never | Judges *rarely* overturn precedent. |
| Degree | very, extremely, fairly, quite | The ruling was *extremely* controversial. |

## 2. Formation
- Most: adjective + *-ly* → *careful → carefully*
- Adjectives ending in *-y* → *-ily*: *easy → easily*
- Adjectives ending in *-le* → *-ly*: *probable → probably*
- Irregular: *good → well*, *fast → fast*, *hard → hard*, *late → late*
- ⚠️ *hardly* is not the adverb of *hard* — it means "barely/almost not": *He hardly worked* ≠ *He worked hard*.

## 3. Adverb Position
The position you choose changes both meaning and naturalness.

| Position | Typical adverbs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Front | time, comment, linking | *Surprisingly*, the appeal was rejected. |
| Mid | frequency, degree, comment | She *often* reviews contracts. He has *already* left. |
| End | manner, place, specific time | She presented the case *clearly* *yesterday*. |

> Mid-position rule: the adverb goes **after** the verb *to be*, **before** other main verbs, and **between** an auxiliary/modal and the main verb.
> - She *is* *usually* punctual.
> - She *usually* arrives early.
> - She *has* *always* *been* committed to the case. (with multiple auxiliaries, the adverb sits after the first one)

## 4. Degree Adverbs & Intensifiers
The right intensifier depends on whether the adjective is **gradable** or **ungradable/extreme** (already at a maximum, like *essential*, *impossible*, *exhausted*).

| Gradable adjectives | Ungradable / extreme adjectives |
|---|---|
| *very / really / quite / fairly / rather* + important, difficult, good | *absolutely / completely / utterly* + essential, impossible, exhausted, certain |

- ✅ *very important* / ❌ ~~very essential~~ → ✅ **absolutely essential**

This pairing is a recurring C1 vocabulary and Use of English point.

## 5. Focusing Adverbs
*only, even, just, particularly* — position changes the emphasis.

- *Only* the judge can decide this. (no one else can)
- The judge can *only* decide this. (this is the limit of her power)

## 6. Linking Adverbs (Conjuncts) — The Classic C1 Punctuation Trap
*however, therefore, moreover, furthermore, nevertheless, nonetheless, consequently, in addition, on the other hand*

These are **adverbs**, not conjunctions — they cannot join two clauses with a comma alone. This is one of the most common errors in CAE Writing and Use of English Part 4.

- ❌ ~~The evidence was weak, however the jury convicted him.~~
- ✅ The evidence was weak. **However**, the jury convicted him.
- ✅ The evidence was weak; **however**, the jury convicted him.
- ✅ The evidence was weak, **but** the jury convicted him. (*but* is a true conjunction, so a comma works here)

> Pattern: full stop or semicolon **before** the linking adverb, comma **after** it.

## 7. Viewpoint & Comment Adverbs
*apparently, frankly, fortunately, surprisingly, undoubtedly, arguably* — express the speaker's attitude to the whole sentence, usually in front position + comma.

- *Arguably*, this is the most important ruling of the decade.
- *Frankly*, the argument doesn't hold up.

## 8. Comparative & Superlative Adverbs
- One-syllable: *fast → faster → fastest*
- *-ly* adverbs: *more/most* + adverb → *carefully → more carefully → most carefully*
- Irregular: *well → better → best*; *badly → worse → worst*

## Common Mistakes

**General**
- Joining two clauses with a linking adverb + comma only (see section 6).
- Pairing intensifiers wrongly with extreme adjectives (*very exhausted* instead of *absolutely exhausted*).
- Misplacing frequency adverbs around *to be*.

**For Portuguese speakers**
- Word order transfer: ❌ "I like very much this book" → ✅ **I like this book very much** (or **I really like this book**).
- *"Porém"* / *"no entanto"* can follow a comma quite naturally in Portuguese, which transfers into English as ❌ "It was raining, however we went out" → ✅ **It was raining; however, we went out.**
- *"Sempre"* sits comfortably before the verb in Portuguese, including before *ser/estar*, which leads to ❌ "I always am tired after class" → ✅ **I am always tired after class.**

## Quick Practice
1. The argument was, however, ___ flawed. (completely / very)
2. Place "always" correctly: *She finishes her reports early.*
3. Fix the punctuation: *The case was strong, nevertheless they lost.*
4. The decision was ___ unexpected. (very / absolutely)
5. Place "only" to mean "no one else can appeal": *the defendant can appeal.*

**Answers:** 1. completely 2. She always finishes her reports early. 3. The case was strong; nevertheless, they lost. 4. very (unexpected is gradable) 5. Only the defendant can appeal.
