## 1. Comparatives & Superlatives (pg 14, 188)

|Type|Short adjectives|Long adjectives|Irregular|
|---|---|---|---|
|Comparative|-er (taller)|more + adj (more difficult)|good→better, bad→worse, far→further/farther|
|Superlative|the -est (tallest)|the most + adj (the most difficult)|the best, the worst, the furthest|

**⚠️ Your recurring error — double comparatives.** Never combine `more` with an `-er` ending: ~~"more better", "more easier"~~. Pick one system, not both. "Better" is already comparative; "more good" doesn't exist.

Other structures worth knowing at C1 level:

- **as...as**: _not as/so expensive as_
- **the...the** (proportional): _The more you practise, the more confident you'll feel._
- **intensifiers**: _far/a lot/much more expensive_ (not "very more expensive"), _slightly/a bit more expensive_
- **comparatives with less**: _less complicated_ (not "more less")

## 2. Pronunciation of Adjectives ending in "-ly"

Words like _friendly, lively, lonely, likely, lovely, silly, ugly, cowardly, deadly_ are **adjectives**, not adverbs, even though they end in -ly (the normal adverb suffix). Common trap: trying to say "friendlily" — it doesn't exist. Use a paraphrase instead: _in a friendly way/manner_.

Pronunciation note: stress stays on the root syllable; the -ly is unstressed /li/. Compare:

- LIVE-ly /ˈlaɪv.li/ (adjective) vs. LIVE (verb, different vowel)
- FRIEND-ly /ˈfrend.li/

## 3. Reported Speech & Reporting Verbs

**What "backshift" actually is:** when you report what someone said, you're describing it from a later point in time — the original words were spoken in the past, so the verb tense inside the reported clause typically moves "one step back" into the past to reflect that gap. It's not a special rule to memorise in isolation; it's just normal sequencing — the same way you'd naturally say "she _was_ tired" (not "she _is_ tired") if you were describing yesterday's dinner. Reported speech applies that same logic to someone's exact words rather than to an event.

The "one step back" ladder looks like this:

|Original tense|Backshifted (reported) tense|
|---|---|
|Present simple|Past simple|
|Present continuous|Past continuous|
|Past simple|Past perfect|
|Present perfect|Past perfect|
|will|would|
|can|could|
|may|might|
|must (obligation)|had to|

Worked examples:

|Direct|Reported|
|---|---|
|"I work here."|She said (that) she worked there.|
|"I am working."|She said she was working.|
|"I have finished."|She said she had finished.|
|"I finished yesterday."|She said she had finished the day before.|
|"I will help."|She said she would help.|
|"I can swim."|She said she could swim.|
|"I may be late."|She said she might be late.|
|"I must leave now."|She said she had to leave then.|

**No backshift needed** when the statement is still true / a general fact (_She said the meeting starts at 9_ — still true now), or when the reporting verb itself is in the present (_She says she's tired_ — no need to shift anything, since you're reporting it in real time).

**Reporting verb patterns** (this is where C1/Advanced tests you — not just "said/told"). Each pattern is fixed to specific verbs; using the wrong pattern with the right verb is the actual C1-level trap, not just knowing the verb's meaning.

- **verb + that (+ clause)**: admit, claim, deny, explain, mention, suggest, insist, argue
    - _He admitted that he had lied._
    - _She claimed (that) she hadn't seen the contract._
    - _The witness mentioned that the door was already open._
- **verb + to-infinitive**: agree, offer, promise, refuse, threaten, decide, plan
    - _He agreed to sign the document._
    - _She promised to call back later._
    - _They refused to comment on the case._
- **verb + object + to-infinitive**: advise, ask, tell, warn, invite, remind, encourage, persuade
    - _The judge advised him to seek legal counsel._
    - _She reminded me to bring the original documents._
    - _They warned us not to enter the building._ (negative: object + not + to-infinitive)
- **verb + gerund**: admit, deny, suggest, recommend, regret, propose, consider
    - _He admitted taking the money._
    - _She denied signing the form._
    - _The lawyer suggested waiting for the official response._
- **verb + preposition + gerund**: insist on, apologise for, accuse (sb) of, congratulate (sb) on, blame (sb) for, warn (sb) against
    - _He insisted on seeing the manager personally._
    - _She apologised for missing the deadline._
    - _They accused him of forging the signature._
    - _The board congratulated her on winning the case._

Quick way to check which pattern a verb takes: try it with a simple filler ("do X") — if "He admitted **to do** it" sounds wrong but "He admitted **doing** it" sounds right, that confirms _admit_ takes the gerund, not the infinitive. This kind of substitution test is faster than memorising lists in the exam.

**Questions**: use reported word order (subject before verb, no inversion), no auxiliary do/did, and _if/whether_ for yes-no questions.

- "Are you coming?" → He asked if I was coming.
- "Did you sign it?" → He asked whether I had signed it.
- "Where do you live?" → He asked where I lived.
- "What time does the office close?" → He asked what time the office closed.

## 4. Conditionals

|Type|Form|Use|
|---|---|---|
|Zero|If + present, present|General truths|
|First|If + present, will + infinitive|Real future possibility|
|Second|If + past simple, would + infinitive|Unreal/hypothetical present or future|
|Third|If + past perfect, would have + past participle|Unreal past (can't change it now)|
|Mixed (past→present)|If + past perfect, would + infinitive|Past condition, present result|
|Mixed (present→past)|If + past simple, would have + past participle|Present/general condition, past result|

Examples of mixed conditionals (these are the ones examiners love at C1):

- _If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now._ (past condition → present result)
- _If she weren't so stubborn, she wouldn't have quit her job._ (present condition → past result)

Alternatives to "if": _unless, provided (that), providing (that), as long as, on condition that, supposing, even if_.

## 5. Possessives

**⚠️ Your recurring error — this/that + plural.** _This/that_ go with singular nouns; _these/those_ go with plural nouns.

- ~~this documents~~ → **these documents**
- ~~that people~~ → **those people**

**'s vs. of:**

- People, animals, time expressions → 's: _the client's request, today's date_
- Things/abstract concepts → of: _the terms of the contract_ (not "the contract's terms" — though this is increasingly accepted in informal English, prefer _of_ in formal/legal writing)

**Double genitive**: _a friend of mine/hers_ (not "a friend of me").

**Possessive with gerund** (formal register): _I appreciate your helping me_ (rather than "you helping me").

## 6. Hypothetical Clauses / I wish / If Only

|Structure|Meaning|
|---|---|
|I wish/If only + past simple|Wish present situation were different|
|I wish/If only + past perfect|Regret about the past|
|I wish/If only + would|Wish someone/something would change (annoyance)|
|It's (high) time + past simple|Something should happen now/already|
|Suppose/imagine + past simple|Hypothesising|
|would rather + past simple (different subject)|Preference about someone else's action|

Examples:

- _I wish I had more time._ (present)
- _If only I hadn't said that._ (past regret)
- _I wish you would stop interrupting._ (annoyance, not "I wish you stopped")
- _It's time we left._

Note: after _wish_, use **were** (not "was") for all persons in formal register: _I wish I were taller._

## 7. Word Formation

Cambridge tests this directly (Reading & Use of English Part 3). Core mechanism: given a root word in capitals, add a prefix/suffix to fit the sentence.

**Common suffixes:**

|Turns into|Examples|
|---|---|
|Noun|-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance/-ence, -ship, -hood|
|Adjective|-able/-ible, -ful, -less, -ive, -al, -ous, -ic|
|Verb|-ise/-ize, -ify, -en|
|Adverb|-ly|
|Negative prefix|un-, in-/im-/il-/ir-, dis-, non-|

**Traps:**

- _responsible → irresponsible_ (ir- before r)
- _legal → illegal_ (il- before l)
- _possible → impossible_ (im- before p, m, b)
- _complete → incomplete_
- Some negatives use _dis-_ or _non-_, not _un-_: _disadvantage, non-negotiable_

**Word class identification** (your own note: "identification of word class"): before choosing the suffix, ask — does the gap need a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb? Look at what surrounds the blank (article/adjective before → noun; verb needed → check subject/object position).

## 8. Participle Clauses ("Fucking Participles")

Fair enough — these are genuinely one of the harder C1 structures. They replace a relative clause or a second clause to sound more formal/concise.

**Present participle (-ing)** — active meaning, ongoing/simultaneous action:

- _Feeling tired, she went to bed early._ (= As she felt tired...)
- _The man standing by the door is my professor._ (= who is standing)

**Past participle (-ed / irregular)** — passive meaning:

- _Written in 1920, the novel is still popular._ (= which was written)
- _Confused by the instructions, he called for help._

**Perfect participle (having + past participle)** — action completed before the main clause:

- _Having finished the report, she left the office._

**Common error to avoid — dangling participles**: the subject of the participle must match the subject of the main clause.

- ❌ _Walking to work, the rain started._ (the rain isn't walking)
- ✅ _Walking to work, I got caught in the rain._

Reduced relative clauses are a subtype: _The report [written] by the intern contained errors_ (= that was written).

## 9. Past Modals of Deduction

| Modal                      | Certainty                                            | Meaning                           |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| must have + pp             | High certainty, positive conclusion                  | _She must have left already._     |
| can't/couldn't have + pp   | High certainty, negative conclusion                  | _He can't have forgotten._        |
| might/may/could have + pp  | Possibility                                          | _They might have missed the bus._ |
| should/shouldn't have + pp | Not deduction — criticism/regret about a past action | _You shouldn't have said that._   |
| needn't have + pp          | Something was done but was unnecessary               | _You needn't have brought wine._  |

Don't confuse **should have** (criticism) with **must have** (deduction) — a very common C1 mix-up.

## 10. Despite and In Spite of

**Why can't you say "despite of"?** Because _despite_ is already a preposition on its own — it doesn't need "of" the way "in spite" does. "In spite" is a fixed noun phrase that requires "of" to link to the next element; _despite_ was borrowed directly as a preposition and never took "of".

Both are followed by a **noun / pronoun / gerund** — never by a full clause with a conjugated verb:

- _Despite the rain, we went out._
- _In spite of being tired, she finished the race._
- ❌ _Despite it rained..._ → ✅ _Despite the fact that it rained... / Although it rained..._

Use **although/even though/though + subject + verb** when you need a full clause.

## 11. Cleft Sentences

Used to add emphasis by restructuring the sentence.

Cleft Sentences are used to give light into one part of the clause.

**It-cleft**: _It was Maria who solved the problem._ (emphasises Maria)

**Wh-cleft (pseudo-cleft)**: _What I need is more time._ / _What surprised me was his reaction._

**All-cleft**: _All I want is a bit of peace._

These are big in Cambridge Writing (C1 Advanced essays/reports) for adding sophistication — but don't overuse them; one or two per essay is enough to show control without sounding mechanical.

## 12. Passives

Form: **be + past participle**.

|Tense|Active|Passive|
|---|---|---|
|Present simple|writes|is written|
|Past simple|wrote|was written|
|Present perfect|has written|has been written|
|Future|will write|will be written|
|Modal|must write|must be written|

**⚠️ Your recurring error — irregular past participles.** These don't follow the -ed pattern; get them exact: write→**written**, break→**broken**, steal→**stolen**, choose→**chosen**, speak→**spoken**, drive→**driven**, take→**taken**, forget→**forgotten**, hide→**hidden**, know→**known**, see→**seen**, give→**given**, show→**shown**, throw→**thrown**, fall→**fallen**, wear→**worn**, tear→**torn**, swear→**sworn**.

**Causative passive** (have/get something done): _I had my document notarised._ — very useful for your legal-writing context.

**Passive with reporting verbs** (formal, common in legal/academic English): _It is believed that... / The property is said to have been abandoned._

## 13. Gerund vs. To-Infinitive

| After...                                                                  | Use                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| enjoy, avoid, admit, suggest, consider, finish, deny, risk, mind, imagine | **gerund**                 |
| want, decide, agree, promise, refuse, plan, afford, manage, hope, offer   | **to-infinitive**          |
| stop, remember, forget, try, regret, need                                 | **both — meaning changes** |

Meaning-change pairs:

- _stop smoking_ (the smoking ends) vs. _stop to smoke_ (pause in order to smoke)
- _remember locking the door_ (memory of a past action) vs. _remember to lock the door_ (don't forget to do it)
- _try opening the window_ (experiment) vs. _try to open the window_ (attempt, possibly failed)

After **prepositions**, always gerund: _interested in learning, good at writing, instead of asking_.

## 14. Countable / Uncountable Nouns (bonus — recurring error)

**Common uncountable nouns that are countable in Portuguese** — this is exactly where L1 interference bites you:

- advice, information, furniture, luggage/baggage, news, research, evidence, equipment, homework, progress, traffic

These take **no plural -s**, no "a/an", and are quantified with _much, a piece of, a bit of, a lot of_:

- ~~"an advice", "informations"~~ → **a piece of advice, some information**

Countable equivalents you might reach for instead: _a suggestion/tip_ (not "an advice"), _a fact/detail_ (not "an information").
