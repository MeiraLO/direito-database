Format: `Front::Back` — compatible with Obsidian's **Spaced Repetition** plugin.

**If the plugin still doesn't detect these cards, check:**

1. Settings → Spaced Repetition → "Flashcard tags" field literally contains `#flashcards` (it's case-sensitive and must match exactly what's used here).
2. The `#flashcards` tag above (line 8) is a real inline tag, not just frontmatter — this is the most reliable trigger.

## Comparatives & Superlatives

What's wrong with "more better"?::Double comparative — never combine "more" with an "-er" ending. Just say "better". Comparative of "far"?::further / farther Fill: This is ___ (bad) mistake I've ever made.::the worst

## Adjectives in -ly

Is "friendly" an adjective or adverb?::Adjective — despite the -ly ending. There's no adverb form; use "in a friendly way". How do you say something was done in a lively manner, using "lively"?::"in a lively way/manner" (not "livelily")

## Reported Speech

"I'll help," she said. → reported::She said (that) she would help. Reporting verb + gerund example with "deny"::She denied stealing the money. Reporting verb + preposition + gerund with "insist"::He insisted on seeing the manager. "Are you coming?" reported with "ask"::He asked if I was coming.

Structure of a mixed conditional (past condition → present result)::If + past perfect, would + infinitive (e.g. "If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.") Alternative to "if" meaning "as long as"::provided (that) / providing (that) / as long as Zero conditional use::General truths / facts always true

## Participle Clauses

"Feeling tired, she went to bed" — what kind of participle?::Present participle (-ing), active meaning "Written in 1920, the novel is still popular" — what kind?::Past participle, passive meaning "Having finished the report, she left" — what kind?::Perfect participle — action completed before the main clause What's a dangling participle?::When the subject of the participle clause doesn't match the subject of the main clause (e.g. "Walking to work, the rain started" — the rain wasn't walking)

## Despite / In Spite of

Why is "despite of" wrong?::"Despite" is already a full preposition on its own and never takes "of" — unlike "in spite of", where "of" links the fixed phrase "in spite" to the noun. Can despite/in spite of be followed by a full clause with a conjugated verb?::No — only noun/pronoun/gerund. Use "although/even though" for a full clause.

## Cleft Sentences

It-cleft for "Maria solved the problem"::It was Maria who solved the problem. Wh-cleft for "I need more time"::What I need is more time.

## Passives

Past participle of "steal"::stolen Past participle of "write"::written Past participle of "choose"::chosen Causative passive: "I ___ my documents notarised."::had (I had my documents notarised)

## Gerund vs Infinitive

"stop smoking" vs "stop to smoke" — difference?::"stop smoking" = the smoking ends; "stop to smoke" = pause in order to smoke "remember locking the door" vs "remember to lock the door"::first = memory of having done it; second = not forgetting to do it (future obligation) Verb pattern after prepositions (e.g. "interested in")::always gerund
