## Comparatives & Superlatives

What's wrong with "more better"?::Double comparative — never combine "more" with an "-er" ending. Just say "better".

Comparative of "far"?::further / farther

Fill: This is ___ (bad) mistake I've ever made.::the worst

## Adjectives in -ly

Is "friendly" an adjective or adverb?::Adjective — despite the -ly ending. There's no adverb form; use "in a friendly way".

How do you say something was done in a lively manner, using "lively"?::"in a lively way/manner" (not "livelily")

## Reported Speech

"I'll help," she said. → reported::She said (that) she would help.

Reporting verb + gerund example with "deny"::She denied stealing the money.

Reporting verb + preposition + gerund with "insist"::He insisted on seeing the manager.

"Are you coming?" reported with "ask"::He asked if I was coming.

## Conditionals

Structure of a mixed conditional (past condition → present result)::If + past perfect, would + infinitive (e.g. "If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.")

Alternative to "if" meaning "as long as"::provided (that) / providing (that) / as long as

Zero conditional use::General truths / facts always true

## Possessives

Fix: "this documents"::these documents

Fix: "a friend of me"::a friend of mine

's or of: "the terms ___ the contract"::of (abstract/thing → use "of" in formal writing)

## Wish / If Only

I wish + past simple = ?::Wish about the present

I wish + past perfect = ?::Regret about the past

I wish + would = ?::Annoyance about someone else's (unchanging) behaviour

Correct: "I wish I ___ (be) taller" formal register::were

## Participle Clauses

"Feeling tired, she went to bed" — what kind of participle?::Present participle (-ing), active meaning

"Written in 1920, the novel is still popular" — what kind?::Past participle, passive meaning

"Having finished the report, she left" — what kind?::Perfect participle — action completed before the main clause

What's a dangling participle?::When the subject of the participle clause doesn't match the subject of the main clause (e.g. "Walking to work, the rain started" — the rain wasn't walking)

## Past Modals of Deduction

must have + pp = ?::High-certainty positive deduction

can't have + pp = ?::High-certainty negative deduction

should have + pp = ?::NOT deduction — criticism/regret about a past action

needn't have + pp = ?::Something was done but was unnecessary

## Despite / In Spite of

Why is "despite of" wrong?::"Despite" is already a full preposition on its own and never takes "of" — unlike "in spite of", where "of" links the fixed phrase "in spite" to the noun.

Can despite/in spite of be followed by a full clause with a conjugated verb?::No — only noun/pronoun/gerund. Use "although/even though" for a full clause.

## Cleft Sentences

It-cleft for "Maria solved the problem"::It was Maria who solved the problem.

Wh-cleft for "I need more time"::What I need is more time.

## Passives

Past participle of "steal"::stolen

Past participle of "write"::written

Causative passive: "I ___ my documents notarised."::had (I had my documents notarised)

## Gerund vs Infinitive

"stop smoking" vs "stop to smoke" — difference?::"stop smoking" = the smoking ends; "stop to smoke" = pause in order to smoke

"remember locking the door" vs "remember to lock the door"::first = memory of having done it; second = not forgetting to do it (future obligation)

Verb pattern after prepositions (e.g. "interested in")::always gerund

## Parts of Speech — Definitions

What is a noun?::A word that names a person, place, thing, idea, or quality (e.g. lawyer, Rio, contract, freedom, kindness).

What is a verb?::A word that expresses an action, state, or occurrence (e.g. run, believe, become, seem).

What is an adjective?::A word that describes or modifies a noun, giving more information about it (e.g. tall, legal, convincing, unjustified).

What is an adverb?::A word that modifies a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or a whole sentence — usually describing how, when, where, or to what degree (e.g. quickly, very, yesterday, unfortunately).

What question does an adjective typically answer about a noun?::"What kind?" or "Which one?" (e.g. the legal document → what kind of document?)

What question does an adverb typically answer about a verb?::"How?", "When?", "Where?", or "To what extent?" (e.g. He argued convincingly → how did he argue?)

Can an adjective modify a verb?::No — adjectives only modify nouns/pronouns. Only adverbs modify verbs.

Give an example of an adverb modifying an adjective (not a verb)::"extremely difficult" — extremely (adverb) modifies difficult (adjective), not a verb.

Difference between "hard" and "hardly" as adverbs?::"hard" = with effort/intensity (he worked hard); "hardly" = almost not (he hardly worked) — a classic false-friend trap, not a regular -ly adverb of "hard".

What part of speech typically comes right after "the" or "a" with nothing in between?::A noun (e.g. the contract, a lawyer) — if an adjective is there instead, it still has to be followed by a noun.

## Parts of Speech — What Class Does the Gap Need?

"This case is highly ___." (COMPLICATE) — what class does the gap need?::Adjective — it follows the linking verb "is" and describes the subject "This case" (→ complicated)

"The board reached its ___ after a long meeting." (DECIDE) — what class does the gap need?::Noun — it follows the possessive "its" and is the object of "reached" (→ decision)

"They ___ agreed to the new terms." (RELUCTANT) — what class does the gap need?::Adverb — it modifies the verb "agreed" (→ reluctantly)

"The report needs to be ___ before submission." (REVISE) — what class does the gap need?::Verb (past participle) — it completes the passive structure "needs to be ___" (→ revised)

"Her argument was surprisingly ___." (CONVINCE) — what class does the gap need?::Adjective — it follows the linking verb "was" and is modified by the adverb "surprisingly" (→ convincing)

"The committee will ___ the proposal next week." (REVIEW) — what class does the gap need?::Verb — it follows the modal "will" and needs the base form (→ review)

"His ___ was clear to everyone in the room." (NERVOUS) — what class does the gap need?::Noun — it's the subject of the sentence, preceded by the possessive "His" (→ nervousness)

"She spoke ___ about the case." (PASSION) — what class does the gap need?::Adverb — it modifies the verb "spoke" (→ passionately)
